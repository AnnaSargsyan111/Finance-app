## T2 (ANSWER)

**Question:** What is compound interest? Show a small example.

**Answer:**

> **Compound interest** is the interest you earn not only on your original principal but also on the interest that has already been added to it.
> 
> *Example*  
> - Start with ₾1,000.  
> - Annual interest = 5 % (compounded once a year).  
> 
> Year 1: ₾1,000 × 0.05 = ₾50 interest → balance = ₾1,050.  
> Year 2: ₾1,050 × 0.05 = ₾52.50 interest → balance = ₾1,102.50.  
> 
> After two years you have ₾1,102.50, not just ₾1,100, because the second year’s interest was calculated on the higher balance.  
> 
> The formula is **Future = Principal × (1 + rate)ⁿ**, where n is the number of compounding periods.

## T5 (ANSWER)

**Question:** What does P/E mean?

**Answer:**

> The **P/E ratio** (price‑to‑earnings) shows how much investors are willing to pay for one unit of a company’s earnings.  
> 
> - **P** = current share price.  
> - **E** = earnings per share (EPS) over the last 12 months.  
> 
> P/E = share price ÷ EPS.  
> 
> A high P/E can mean the market expects strong future growth, while a low P/E may suggest the stock is cheap or that earnings are expected to fall. It’s just one tool; you should also look at other factors before deciding.
